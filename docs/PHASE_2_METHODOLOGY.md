# Phase 2 methodology and provenance

## Question and identification

The question is the cumulative probability of reactivation within 3, 6, or 12 months from an assumed prediction origin. Empirical time-to-event modeling would need an account ID, pre-event covariates, an entry date, an observed reactivation timestamp or duration, and a censoring endpoint with a defensible observation mechanism. The supplied 2,000-row extract has no reactivation event histories or censoring endpoints. Every supplied booking date is deterministic from account ID. Consequently, no Everpure Cox coefficient, baseline survival, calibration, or predictive performance can be estimated from it.

The implementation uses four provenance classes: `OBSERVED_SOURCE` for unchanged CSV fields; `DERIVED_SOURCE` for Phase 1 cleaning, hierarchy, and cohort rules; `SYNTHETIC_METHODOLOGY` for the simulated event histories and Cox estimates; `ASSUMPTION_BASED_NOT_CALIBRATED` for the scenario hazards, effects, probabilities, and counts. The output manifest identifies each file. Source fields are never used as fabricated events.

## Lane A: synthetic Cox demonstration

`survival_synthetic.generate_synthetic_cohort` uses NumPy's default generator with seed 20261009 and 1,600 independent invented subjects. Every subject enters at synthetic study enrollment, t=0. A month is a continuous simulation unit, not a specific calendar month. Covariates are health proxy ~ Bernoulli(0.4), engagement proxy ~ Normal(0,1), and partner proxy ~ Bernoulli(0.5). The generating baseline hazard is 0.035 per month. Generating log-hazard coefficients are +0.45, -0.35, and +0.25, respectively. The data-generating hazard is `0.035 exp(beta'x)` and true time is sampled from the equivalent exponential distribution. An independent censoring time is Uniform(8,48) months capped administratively at 36 months. Observed duration is `min(true event time, censoring time)`; the event flag is 1 exactly when event time is at or before censoring. True event and censor times exist in the generator and its tests but are excluded from the exported fitting cohort and model matrix.

`fit_synthetic_cox` validates unique synthetic IDs, positive finite durations, binary events, finite full-rank covariates, and a condition-number bound. It rejects an all-censored fit. Lifelines `CoxPHFitter` fits the observed duration, event flag, and three pre-event covariates. Lifelines convergence warnings and numerical runtime warnings are captured and published; either makes `converged=false` with `fit_status=warning_unstable`. A hard numerical fitting exception instead raises `SyntheticCoxFitError` with `fit_status=hard_failure`. The fitted baseline survival is checked to be in [0,1] and nonincreasing. Lifelines' baseline survival is at its mean covariate profile, not the all-zero profile, which is separately predicted. Rank-transformed Schoenfeld residual tests provide proportional-hazards diagnostics; a rejection is reported and does not trigger seed shopping. The in-sample concordance and concordance on an independently generated 400-subject synthetic holdout are separate. The Kaplan–Meier curve, fitted coefficients, hazard ratios, 95% confidence intervals, baseline survival, and synthetic profile survival/event probabilities are exported. Confidence intervals reflect synthetic sampling, never uncertainty in the Everpure assumptions.

Cox partial likelihood estimates relative effects without specifying the baseline hazard. Absolute survival predictions require estimating baseline survival. Censoring means the event was unobserved through follow-up, not that the subject never reactivates. Hazard ratios are conditional instantaneous associations, not cumulative probabilities or causal effects.

## Lane B: account scenarios

`reactivation_scenarios` imports no Lane A generator, fitted coefficient, model object, or fixture. `load_phase1_accounts` checks the authoritative source SHA, source rows against the locally generated cleaned CSV, Phase 1 analysis date, 2,000-row account grain, one-to-one hierarchy/quality joins, root mapping, ticket sentinel policy, and reported inactivity rule. The primary `reported_2y_scenario_cohort` contains 1,144 accounts with source-reported `years_inactive >= 2`. The 562 eligibility conflicts and 294 unknowns are retained in `cohort_reconciliation.csv`. This policy does not confirm the brief's two-fiscal-year eligibility. The 2,000 rows are the supplied extract, not a proven complete install base.

Only four interpretable feature groups enter the assumed linear predictor: health Red/Yellow indicators versus Green, partner-managed yes, log(1+valid support tickets), and child versus root position. Missing values retain explicit flags and receive a **reference-level assumed contribution with explicit missingness flags**: unknown health scores like Green, unknown partner ownership like direct, and missing tickets like zero valid tickets. Source ticket 999 stays missing and actual zero tickets stays zero. These scoring choices do not correct source data or establish an observed business effect. Historical spend, monthly usage, reported inactivity amount, account ID, booking date, contract date, and discount tier are excluded from the hazard model. Historical spend is not current revenue, ARR, or recoverable cash; hierarchy spend is not economically consolidated. No treatment response is inferred from discount tier.

The [assumption register](PHASE_2_ASSUMPTIONS.json) holds all parameters under `phase2-assumptions-v1`: common log-hazard effects of -0.35 (Red), -0.15 (Yellow), +0.15 (partner managed), -0.08 per log(1+tickets), and -0.10 (child); monthly baseline hazards of 0.006, 0.012, and 0.020 for conservative, base, and optimistic. These are illustrative choices, not learned effects or conversion benchmarks. The scenario origin is 2026-10-09 by convention; there is no evidence a campaign started then. With `lambda_s` the assumed baseline and `eta_i` the assumed predictor, `S_i,s(t)=exp[-lambda_s t exp(eta_i)]` and `P_i,s(T<=t)=1-S_i,s(t)`. All durations and hazards use continuous months. Ordered baselines and common effects guarantee ordered account probabilities.

Expected reactivations equal the sum of 1,144 account probabilities at each scenario and horizon. The count is conditional on assumptions. Linearity of expectation holds even if accounts in a root group are dependent; dependence matters to the variance/distribution of the count. No variance or confidence interval is estimated for the business scenarios. Hypothetical policies include conflicts (1,706 accounts) or conflicts plus unknowns (2,000), with separate denominators and labels in sensitivity output; they do not overwrite the primary export. Baseline-hazard and coefficient-scale changes keep the primary denominator fixed, making propensity changes distinguishable from population changes.

Missing-health sensitivity also keeps the primary 1,144-account denominator fixed. Unknown health is separately assigned the chosen Yellow or Red log effect, or a weighted mean of the chosen Green/Yellow/Red log effects using their **observed frequencies among known-health accounts in the primary cohort**. The observed frequencies do not estimate the effects. This sensitivity does not alter the primary prediction export and cannot prove missingness is ignorable. Customer-level ranking may change even if the aggregate expectation remains in a nearby range.

## Execution and boundaries

`phase2_pipeline.run_scenario_lane` runs independently of Lane A; `run_pipeline` invokes it and then executes the synthetic lane. The account export contains no synthetic identifiers; the synthetic cohort contains no real account IDs. Account-level probability and cohort-reconciliation CSVs are generated locally but ignored by Git; the executed notebook shows only anonymous synthetic/hypothetical profiles and aggregate account results. Stable row sorting, fixed seeds, fixed float serialization, sorted JSON keys, and a file-hash manifest make repeat runs byte-identical in the tested environment. This does not promise cross-platform floating-point byte identity. The notebook calls the actual modules in a fresh kernel and creates six labeled figures. No Phase 3 financial simulation, Phase 4 routing, or Phase 5 presentation is implemented.
